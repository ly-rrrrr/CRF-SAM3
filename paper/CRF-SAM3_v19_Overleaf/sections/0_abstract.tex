\begin{abstract}
Referring video object segmentation (RVOS) requires both reliable target discovery and fine-grained referent discrimination. We argue that these two requirements need not be solved at the same semantic granularity: a compact object concept is often sufficient to expose plausible trajectories, whereas the final decision may depend on attributes, actions, relations, and temporal events. We therefore propose CRF-SAM3, a \emph{Ground--Recall--Verify} framework. An expression-conditioned target-query module first learns a referent-aware open-vocabulary recall concept from frozen Qwen3-VL visual tokens. Ground-truth masks supervise query responses only during training, while inference uses the referring expression and RGB frames alone. The grounded concept then prompts SAM3 to recall multiple temporally coherent candidate tubes, deliberately preserving ambiguity. Finally, a Qwen3-VL verifier evaluates the recalled tubes under the full expression and is trained with listwise competition, hard-negative ranking, and mask-aware feature alignment. Across four benchmarks, CRF-SAM3 obtains 71.45 $\mathcal{J}\&\mathcal{F}$ on MeViS val$^u$, -- on Ref-DAVIS17, -- on Ref-Youtube-VOS, and 59.74 on ReVOS. Candidate-space and verifier analyses further show that recall coverage and semantic discrimination are complementary and separately measurable bottlenecks.
\end{abstract}
